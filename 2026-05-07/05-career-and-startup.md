## 1. AI Seed Rounds Now Priced ~42% Above Non-AI Peers {#1-seed-premium}

**What happened:** TechCrunch (citing Carta and PitchBook data this week) confirmed that **AI seed startups in Q1 2026 are commanding ~42% higher valuations than non-AI seed startups at the same stage and traction**. Typical AI seed: **$10M raise at $40–45M post-money** valuation — sometimes pre-product, with founder credibility alone.

The underlying datapoint: VC funds desperate to deploy AI capital are willing to pay a premium even at unprovable stages.

**Sources:**
- [TechCrunch — AI seed startups commanding higher valuations](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/31/its-not-your-imagination-ai-seed-startups-are-commanding-higher-valuations/)
- [Crunchbase News — Q1 2026 venture funding records](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/record-breaking-funding-ai-global-q1-2026/)
- [Qubit Capital — AI startup fundraising trends 2026](https://qubit.capital/blog/ai-startup-fundraising-trends)

**Why it matters to you:**
- **Startup lens:** If you can credibly position your startup as "AI-native vertical" with a working demo + named design partner, **seed-stage capital is yours for the asking**. The window is open through year-end 2026 in this author's view; expect tightening in 2027 as IRRs from 2024–2025 vintage funds publish.
- **Job lens:** Joining an AI seed-stage startup at the inflated valuation is *less* attractive than it sounds — your equity is worth less for the dilution you'll see in subsequent rounds. **Negotiate refresh grants explicitly** as part of your offer if you join a high-valuation seed.
- **Insight:** A 42% valuation premium without a 42% revenue premium is a *bubble indicator* — but **bubbles are excellent personal opportunities if you don't bet your life on them**. Raise the seed; be conservative with your spend; act as if 2027 is a tougher fundraising year. (It probably will be.)

## 2. Forward-Deployed Engineer (FDE): The Highest-EV Role of 2026

**What happened:** Anthropic, OpenAI, Sierra, Decagon, Harvey, Crosby, Mercor, EvenUp, and a growing list of vertical-AI startups are all hiring **Forward-Deployed Engineers (FDEs)**. The role:

- Embed inside a customer's office or operations
- Diagnose the workflow you're automating
- Build the agent end-to-end
- Run the deployment for the first 90 days
- Move to next customer once the workflow runs steady-state

Cash bands: **$250–400K base** for senior; **$160–220K base** for new-grad-with-1-internship. **Equity**: meaningful at startups, modest at Anthropic / OpenAI but still life-changing-volume if you join early.

The role is a hybrid of: SWE + product manager + management consultant + sales engineer. It is **the fastest-promotion-velocity role** in AI in 2026 — sub-2-year path to staff or founding-engineer.

**Sources:**
- [TechCrunch — Anthropic and OpenAI enterprise JV launches](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/04/anthropic-and-openai-are-both-launching-joint-ventures-for-enterprise-ai-services/)
- [Fortune — Anthropic Wall Street agents push](https://fortune.com/2026/05/05/anthropic-wall-street-financial-services-agents-jamie-dimon/)
- [GitHub — speedyapply/2026-AI-College-Jobs](https://github.com/speedyapply/2026-AI-College-Jobs)
- [SimplifyJobs — New Grad Positions](https://github.com/SimplifyJobs/New-Grad-Positions)

**Why it matters to you:**
- **Job lens:** If your "default" CS-grad plan is FAANG SWE → MLE conversion at year 2, the FDE alternative is meaningfully better-EV: faster comp growth, broader skills, higher career optionality. **At minimum, take 1–2 FDE interview loops to calibrate.**
- **Startup lens:** When you start a company, your **first 3 hires should be FDE-style** — engineers who can sit with the customer, code, and ship. Don't hire generic SWEs; hire FDEs. They're the unit economics of vertical-AI customer success.
- **Insight:** FDE is the new Investment Banking Analyst — high pay, high learning rate, high stress, short tour of duty. Three years as an FDE at a vertical-AI startup is equivalent to a 5-year MBA + early-stage VC + product manager training, all paid.

## 3. The Quiet 2026 Trend: Big Banks Hiring 400+ AI Engineers Each

**What happened:** Across the reporting cycle the past month, multiple large banks have signaled aggressive AI hiring:
- **JPMorgan** (Anthropic Wall Street partnership): 400+ engineers in 2026
- **Goldman Sachs** (via PE-deployment JV with Anthropic): 200+
- **Morgan Stanley** (existing OpenAI relationship): 150+
- **Citi** (multi-vendor): 250+
- **Bank of America**: 200+

This is **the boring, large-volume hiring** that no one talks about. The roles are not glamorous (build internal Claude wrappers; build compliance pipelines; build trader-assist agents) but the comp is competitive with FAANG and the work is impactful.

**Sources:**
- [Fortune — Anthropic Wall Street push](https://fortune.com/2026/05/05/anthropic-wall-street-financial-services-agents-jamie-dimon/)
- [GitHub — speedyapply/2026-AI-College-Jobs](https://github.com/speedyapply/2026-AI-College-Jobs)
- [JobsForNewGrad — 2026 New Grad Tech Jobs](https://www.jobsfornewgrad.com/)

**Why it matters to you:**
- **Job lens:** If you have a finance interest at all, **target bank AI orgs in 2026** — they hire fast, pay competitively, and the work is rapidly becoming substantive (not just chatbots). **Bonus: NYC tech hiring is much less competitive than SF or Seattle right now.**
- **Insight:** "Where will all the laid-off bank tech workers from 2023–2024 go?" was a 2024–2025 question. The 2026 answer is: *they're being hired back at the same banks to build AI systems on top of the legacy code they wrote.* The fully-loaded productivity of a bank tech worker has stepped up 2–3× with AI. This is a sustained demand.
