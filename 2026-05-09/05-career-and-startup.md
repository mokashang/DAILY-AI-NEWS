## 1. The Cloudflare Layoffs Tell You What's Safe vs. What's Not

**The signal:** Cloudflare cut **1,100 people (~20% of headcount)** at **+25% YoY revenue and a record quarter** (May 7 earnings). CEO Matthew Prince's stated rationale: *"the productivity gains from the people directly talking to customers and directly creating code have been incredible, and a lot of the support roles behind them are not going to be the roles that drive companies going forward."*

**Read the rationale carefully — it's prescriptive about which jobs are at risk:**

| Protected (per Cloudflare) | At risk (per Cloudflare) |
|---|---|
| Customer-facing engineers | Internal-tooling support |
| People who write production code | Mid-tier QA / process management |
| People talking to customers | Back-office ops |
| Builders | Anyone whose work is well-documented and repeatable |

**The market punished Cloudflare with a 24% stock drop the next day.** That doesn't mean the layoffs were wrong; it means investors don't yet trust that the productivity gains are real and durable. **Watch Q2 earnings — if Cloudflare's revenue/employee jumps, every public software CFO will copy this. If it doesn't, the AI-productivity narrative takes a public hit.**

**What you should do this week:**
1. **Audit your résumé** for words that map to "support role" — internal-tools-focused, process-improvement, documentation-only. Rewrite those bullets to emphasize **customer impact** or **shipped code**.
2. **Take on at least one customer-facing project** in your current research lab or internship — a tool, a paper deployment, a tutorial users actually use. Customer-facing experience is the moat.
3. **Decide your bet:** if you believe Prince, focus your skill stack on building/customer/ML eng. If you don't, hedge into roles AI can't easily touch (research, sales engineering, security clearance work).

## 4. The 2026 Founder Wedge: Vertical Agents in Mid-Market Regulated Industries

**The pattern, evidenced this week:**
- **Sierra** owns Fortune 50 customer-AI ($15.8B) — but has zero presence in mid-market
- **Anthropic Wall Street** push targets the **top investment banks** — leaves regional banks, RIAs, hedge funds <$500M AUM open
- **Novo Nordisk + OpenAI** is a Big Pharma deal — leaves mid-cap pharma, biotech, contract research orgs uncovered
- **Pentagon picks 8 vendors for classified AI** — leaves state and local government wide open

**The wedge formula:**

> Big enterprise + frontier lab partnership = the top of the pyramid is locked.
> Mid-market + same workflows + smaller deal sizes = the open pyramid base.

**Why this is the right wedge for a CS grad founder, specifically:**
- Mid-market deal sizes ($30K–$200K ACV) are **closeable in <90 days** without a sales team
- **Regulatory complexity** in finance/healthcare/legal is a moat against generic ChatGPT-wrapper competitors
- **Workflow specificity** beats raw capability — knowing *how* a regional bank moves a mortgage from intake to underwriting is more valuable than +5% on a benchmark

## 5. Compute, Capital, and Your Career — How to Read the $100B+ Compute Deals

**This week's compute math:**
- Anthropic: $200B Google + $100B+ AWS + 100% of Colossus 1 + ARR ~$7B ⇒ **>40× revenue locked into compute commitments**
- OpenAI: $122B raise at $852B + $50B Amazon + $30B NVIDIA + $30B SoftBank
- Pentagon: 8-vendor multi-cloud AI procurement framework

**What this tells you about your career:**

1. **Compute is being allocated by relationship, not market.** The frontier labs aren't picking the cheapest cloud — they're picking the *politically reliable* one. As an employee or founder, your access to frontier capability now depends on which lab you're aligned with.

2. **Inference cost is collapsing for everyone except the top of the market.** Open-weights frontier (DeepSeek V4, Kimi K2.6) is 10–20× cheaper than Anthropic Opus on similar tasks. **Your startup's unit economics improve by switching providers, not by raising more capital.**

3. **The "frontier moat" is no longer a moat for products, only for labs.** A startup using Claude Opus 4.7 for a workflow has roughly the same product as one using DeepSeek V4 — what differs is **margin** and **distribution**. Margin you can engineer; distribution you have to earn.

4. **The infra job market is hotter than the model-building job market.** Demand for ML systems engineers, accelerator specialists (TPU, Trainium, GB200), distributed-training engineers vastly exceeds the supply. **As a CS grad, the leverage point is "I can debug a CUDA OOM at 3am" or "I can profile and optimize an SGLang deployment."** That's a more reliable career bet than "I trained a small reasoning model on my GPU."
