## 1. Isomorphic Labs Closes $2.1B Series B — Largest Private AI-Drug-Discovery Round Ever {#1-isomorphic}

**What happened:** **Isomorphic Labs** — the DeepMind spinout founded by Demis Hassabis to commercialize AlphaFold-class AI for drug discovery — **closed a $2.1B Series B on May 12, 2026**, led by **Thrive Capital**, with participation from existing backers **Alphabet** and **GV**, plus new investors **MGX (Abu Dhabi sovereign AI fund), Temasek (Singapore sovereign), CapitalG (Alphabet's growth fund), and the UK Sovereign AI Fund.**

- **Round leader:** Thrive Capital
- **Total capital base:** ~$2.6B
- **Use of funds:** Continued development of the **IsoDDE (Isomorphic Drug Design Engine)**, expansion of the therapeutic-program pipeline toward clinical-stage, and hiring across **London + Cambridge MA + Lausanne (Switzerland)**
- **Named pharma partners:** **Novartis, Eli Lilly, Johnson & Johnson** (collaboration deals predate this round but anchor commercial validation)

**Why it's bigger than the headline:** This is the **first AI-vertical round with a four-corner cap table** — model lab (DeepMind heritage) + tier-1 VC (Thrive, GV, CapitalG) + sovereign-AI funds (MGX, Temasek, UK) + big-pharma collaboration (Novartis, Lilly, J&J). Each corner brings something the others can't: DeepMind brings model + research talent; Thrive brings the playbook for scaling from research-stage to commercial-grade; sovereigns bring multi-decade patient capital + national-strategic-priority alignment; big-pharma brings clinical pipelines, regulatory pathways, and the eventual exit / IPO comp set. **This template — Lab + VC + Sovereign + Industry — is likely to be replicated in 2026–2027 across biotech, materials science, robotics, and energy.**

The **UK Sovereign AI Fund** entry is the most novel element. The UK has been moving deliberately to position itself as the "AI sovereignty layer between EU and US" since 2025; this is its first headline AI-vertical anchor investment.

## 2. Anthropic $30B Raise at $900B+ — Still No Term Sheet as of Monday Evening {#2-anthropic-raise}

## 3. Ramp AI Index — Three Enumerable Threats to Anthropic's 2.1pt Lead {#3-ramp-threats}

**Context:** Friday May 13, **Ramp's May AI Index** showed Anthropic at **34.4%** US-business AI adoption vs OpenAI's **32.3%** — the first time Anthropic has led on this metric. The headline drowned out the **three explicit risks** the Ramp analysts and follow-up VentureBeat / Axios coverage identified. Worth reading carefully — these are the most credible *near-term* downside scenarios on the Anthropic adoption story.

### Threat 1: Incentive misalignment between Anthropic and its buyers
- **Anthropic monetizes tokens.** Higher revenue ⇔ buyers consume more tokens.
- **Buyers want cost-efficient AI.** Cheaper, faster models that finish a task with fewer tokens.
- **Anthropic's selling motion is therefore (subtly) biased toward Opus over Haiku, Cowork over plain API, longer-context tasks over shorter ones.**
- The June 15 Agent SDK metering change *makes this worse*, because the metering line item makes buyers acutely aware that Anthropic's revenue scales linearly with their consumption.

### Threat 2: Cheap open-source inference platforms rising fast on Ramp's own data
- **Together AI, Groq, Fireworks, and similar inference platforms** appeared in the top-fastest-growing AI vendors on Ramp for April 2026.
- These platforms serve **open-source models (Llama, DeepSeek, Mistral, Qwen)** at 10–30% the cost of Claude or GPT for many workloads.
- **Translation: businesses are routing the *cost-sensitive* portion of their AI workload to open-source-on-cheap-inference, while keeping the *capability-sensitive* portion on Claude / GPT.** Anthropic gets the high-margin tasks but a shrinking share of total AI dollars per customer.

### Threat 3: Claude Code is the single product carrying most of Anthropic's YoY adoption growth
- The Ramp analysts identify **Claude Code as the engine** behind Anthropic's adoption pull-ahead.
- Single-product concentration is *fast revenue growth* and *concentration risk* in the same breath.
- **If Cursor, Devin, GitHub Copilot, or — most pointed — Google's *new tomorrow* Vertex agent-coding stack ship something competitive**, Claude Code growth decelerates and the adoption lead narrows.

## 4. The Most Under-Built Lane Among the "Suleyman 4" — Project Management Agents {#4-pm-wedge}

Cross-link with [`01-big-lab-moves.md` §3 Suleyman framing](./01-big-lab-moves.md#3-openai). Among the four white-collar categories Mustafa Suleyman named as first-to-be-automated (accounting · legal · marketing · PM), **PM has the least vertical-agent investment to date.**

### Market map as of May 18, 2026

| Vertical | Vertical-agent leaders | Funding stage |
|---|---|---|
| **Accounting (AR/AP)** | Fazeshift, Ramp/Brex agents, Performativ, Marloo | Series A+ to growth |
| **Legal** | Anthropic Claude for Legal (CoCounsel, Lexis, Thomson Reuters), Harvey, Spellbook, Hebbia | Multiple at $1B+ |
| **Marketing** | Nectar Social, Jasper (agentic), Writer agents, Mutiny | Series A+ |
| **Project Management** | **No category leader yet.** Linear AI features, Asana AI features, Notion AI agents — all *embedded in existing PM tools*, none category-disruptive | Pre-category |

**Why PM has been the slowest of the four:**
- PM work is **less structured than accounting**, less domain-data-rich than legal, and less marketing-style-text-generation than marketing — so generic-LLM agents don't immediately deliver wins.
- The *target user* (PMs, EMs, founders, ops leads) is **non-buyer-rich** at SMB scale; selling into orgs requires Enterprise-grade integrations + change-management.
- The legacy software incumbents (Asana, Atlassian/Jira, Linear, Monday, ClickUp, Notion) are **embedding agents into their existing surface** — which competes with but does not yet *replace* the workflow.

**Where the wedge is:**
- **Async-coding-agent-aware PM** — given the OpenAI Codex Mobile + Anthropic Cowork rise of async coding agents (see [yesterday's `01` §3](../2026-05-17/01-big-lab-moves.md#3-codex-mobile)), every existing PM tool treats "agent" as a 3rd-class artifact. The next $1B PM-agent company treats "agent" as a *first-class assignee with cost, latency, success-rate, and approval fields.*
- **Outcome-priced PM** — sell on *projects shipped*, not *seats licensed*. The Stripe-of-PM thesis (basis points of project value, not flat per-seat license).
- **Vertical-PM agents** — *PM-for-construction*, *PM-for-clinical-trials*, *PM-for-marketing-agencies* — each large enough to be a $300M–$1B business standalone.

**Why it matters to you:**
- **Job lens:** If you join *one of the existing PM tool incumbents* (Linear, Notion, Asana, Atlassian) as an FDE or AI engineer, you are likely working on **defensive embedded-agent features**, not category-disruption. Higher ceiling — but the much harder lane — is to **join a pre-launch PM-agent startup at the founding-engineer-3 level.** Pattern-match the 2012–2015 vertical-SaaS founder pool for the *non-obvious* PMs who'll start the agent-PM equivalent of Asana/Linear; warm-intro the candidates. (Tactical hint: look for senior PMs at YC W22-W25 startups who are now between gigs; the "I built the PM process at a hyper-growth startup for 3 years" cohort is the right talent for this founding-team play.)
- **Startup lens:** This is **the single most actionable wedge from this edition.** The 1-hour exercise: pick one PM-agent thesis from the three sub-categories above, write a 1-page memo, and find one **buyer-side prototype customer** by Friday. If a PM at a 50–200 person company *will pay you* $99/month for an "agent-as-assignee" tool you ship in 4 weekends, you have validated the wedge. Convert that paid prototype into your YC application by the next batch deadline.
- **Insight:** The pattern across the four Suleyman verticals is **legal led, accounting followed, marketing is now actively consolidating, PM is wide open.** This sequencing isn't accidental — it tracks **the order in which the verticals are most rule-bound** (legal: extremely; accounting: extremely; marketing: moderately; PM: least). The least-rule-bound vertical is the *latest* to consolidate because LLM agents need slightly more reliability and richer structured context to deliver wins there. With Mem0 / EverMemOS / structured-memory architectures shipping in production right now, **PM-agent reliability hits the threshold for category-defining product in 2026.** If you are going to pick a wedge this week, this is the one I'd pick.
