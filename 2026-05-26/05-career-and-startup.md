## 1. The AI-hardware / on-device-ML lane {#1-hardware-lane}

**The setup:** Hark ($6B, chipmaker-backed, [`02` §1](./02-new-emerging.md#1-hark)) + OpenAI's io device bet + Apple's AI reboot = a forming market for **AI-native hardware** — which needs engineers most software-only candidates can't supply.

**The crossover skills it pays for:**
- **On-device / edge inference** — running capable models under latency, memory, and power constraints.
- **Model compression** — quantization, distillation, pruning (your cost-and-memory work, [2026-05-26/03 §1](./03-practical-skills-and-tools.md#1-memory-pattern), is *adjacent* — both are "do more with less compute").
- **Embedded systems × ML** — the hardware/software boundary where most ML grads are weak.

**The move:** even if you don't pivot to hardware, **add one on-device project** to your portfolio — e.g., run a small quantized model locally and benchmark latency/cost vs. the API. It signals the crossover and connects directly to your existing efficiency narrative.

### Why it matters to you
- **Job lens:** Thinner queue, higher pay, fit-screened (like defense and security AI). The barrier *is* the opportunity — fewer applicants can do it.
- **Startup lens:** Don't build the device; build the **agents/skills layer that runs on it.** New form factors reset the app layer — watch for the SDK and be a first-mover.
- **Insight:** The efficiency skills you're building for *cost reasons* (memory pruning, cheap-model routing, test-time-compute budgeting) are the **same skills hardware needs for power/latency reasons.** One skill investment, two markets — software-cost and on-device.
