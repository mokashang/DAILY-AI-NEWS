## 2. The 2026 coding-jobs bifurcation is now empirical — and what it means for new-grads {#2-coding-jobs-bifurcation}

**The numbers (this week's read):**

- **142,000 tech layoffs in 2026 YTD** (Meta 8K on May 20, Intuit 3K announced same day; profitable companies cutting to fund **$700B AI infra**)
- **$700B+ combined 2026 hyperscaler capex** — **nearly 2× 2025**
- **AI skills in 42% of all software JDs** (vs **8% in 2022** — ~6× in five years)
- **Software-developer employment for workers under 26: down ~20% since 2024** (Stanford HAI)
- **Roles in acute shortage:** ML infrastructure, model evaluation, AI safety, applied research, FDE, AI Integration, data engineering
- **Roles in contraction:** traditional SWE, PM, recruiting, HR, back-office

**The defining feature** (per multiple analysts this week): **simultaneity of record layoffs and record capex**. Companies are *redirecting* not retreating. Meta's 8K cut + ~7K redirected into Applied AI Engineering, AI Agent Transformation Accelerator, Central Analytics — *as covered [2026-05-20/01](../2026-05-20/01-big-lab-moves.md).*

**The new-grad implication (specific, not generic):**

| Old assumption | 2026 reality |
|---|---|
| "Get a SWE job at FAANG and ladder up" | FAANG SWE under 26 down ~20% since 2024; SWE *with applied-AI proof of work* still climbing |
| "Generalist > specialist for first job" | The 42%-of-JDs stat means **applied-AI is the generalist track now**; "pure SWE" is the new specialist |
| "Just keep applying broad" | **Targeted FDE / AI Integration / Solutions** applications convert 5–10× higher than generic SWE |
| "Layoffs mean a bad market" | Layoffs *plus* $700B capex = a **redistribution**, not a recession. **Apply where the capex flows.** |
