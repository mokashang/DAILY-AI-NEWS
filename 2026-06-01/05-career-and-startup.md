## 1. Comp refresh — AI engineer + FDE numbers from May 2026 {#1-comp-refresh}

**The May 2026 numbers:**

**AI Engineer (general)**
- **Average US**: ~**$206K**. Most-frequently-cited band: **$160K–$200K** (~34% of postings).
- **Senior specialists**: **$200K–$312K** in the US.
- **LLM fine-tuning specialists**: **25–40% above** the $160K US AI median.
- **AI-skill wage premium**: **56%** vs. baseline — **up from 25% last year** (the premium more than doubled in 12 months).
- **Postings increasingly require**: **RAG architecture, LLM evaluation, AI safety**. **75%+** of AI postings now require **deep domain expertise in one area**, not generalist breadth.

**Forward-Deployed Engineer (FDE) — the specific lane this archive has been tracking since [2026-05-17](../2026-05-17/05-career-and-startup.md)**
- **Postings up 800% YoY** (largest 2026 hiring-curve outside of frontier-lab research).
- **Median base salary (April 2026, n=111 disclosed of 134 active)**: **$135K**. **Average**: $139,470. **Range**: $53K → **$1.2M TC**.
- **Google / KPMG / ServiceNow senior FDE base**: **$200K–$365K**.
- **Top tier FDE (frontier-lab Solutions / Customer Engineering)**: **$450K+ TC**, with outliers up to **$1.2M TC**.

## 3. The skill that keeps appreciating: eval / verification / cost-control discipline {#3-eval-as-discipline}

**The convergent evidence from the May-29/30/31 arc + today:**

- [`04` §1 Efficient Benchmarking](./04-research-progress.md#1-efficient-benchmarking): agent rankings preserved at **44–70% lower eval cost** by targeting the 30–70% pass-rate band.
- [`04` §2 AstaBench / AutoResearchBench](./04-research-progress.md#2-astabench-autoresearch): rigorous real-environment benchmarks are now the eval *standard*.
- [`04` §3 Rethinking RL for LLM Reasoning](./04-research-progress.md#3-rl-reasoning): RL's effect is *sparse and identifiable in advance* — i.e., **knowing where to look matters more than running more eval**.
- [`03` §1 Opus 4.8 routing + cost log](./03-practical-skills-and-tools.md#1-opus-48-routing): cost-per-successful-task is the metric that decides production agent design.
- [`02` §4 Cursor Composer 2.5](./02-new-emerging.md#4-cursor-composer): Cursor proved **a fine-tuned in-house model can match Opus 4.7 on coding at ~6× lower cost** — which is *also* an eval/routing-discipline win, not just a training win.

**The pattern:** every major story from the May-29/30/31 arc, on every category file, **routes back to "the eval/verification/cost-control layer is the durable skill."** This is the third consecutive week the archive has surfaced this — at this point it's not a *trend*, it's a *career thesis*.

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** The portfolio artifact that **wins June interviews** is the one that demonstrates *measurement-under-model-churn* — a re-runnable eval harness that captures **cost-per-task + quality + latency** across (at minimum) **two models on two providers**. If you have one such artifact at the top of your GitHub, you read as a 2026-current AI engineer; without one, you read as 2024. **Build the harness this week**, ship the cost log ([`03` §1](./03-practical-skills-and-tools.md#1-opus-48-routing)), and commit it publicly.
- **Startup lens:** This is also a **fundable wedge** — "the Datadog/Sentry of agent reliability" still doesn't have a clear category winner ([Judgment Labs from 2026-05-13](../2026-05-13/02-new-emerging.md) is one of the contenders, but the category is broad). The startup question to ask: **could I be the agent-eval / cost-routing observability play for a specific vertical** (legal agents, healthcare agents, finance agents)? Vertical eval-as-a-product is *thinner* than the horizontal version and easier for a small team to win.
- **Insight:** The deepest version of this thesis is: **as models commoditize, the *skill* that's still scarce is *picking which model for which task, and proving the pick was right*.** That skill is **agent eval + cost-routing + verification**. Three years from now, "I can use Claude" will be a non-statement; "I can pick the optimal model for any task and prove it with a cost log" will still be hireable.

## 4. The brand-new lane: Mythos-adjacent / cleared-evaluator / agentic-SOC {#4-mythos-cyber-lane}

**The lane that didn't exist on 2026-05-22:** With **Mythos going GA in coming weeks** ([`01` §3](./01-big-lab-moves.md#3-mythos-ga)) and the **AI EO returning with NSA-in-the-loop for classified pre-deployment testing** ([`01` §4](./01-big-lab-moves.md#4-eo-nsa)), three adjacent hiring lanes lit up simultaneously:

1. **Cleared AI evaluators** (NSA / NIST AISI / cleared contractors — Booz Allen, Leidos, SAIC, MITRE, RAND). If you have *any* path to clearance, this is the *thinnest* AI hiring lane in 2026 by applicant volume — most AI engineers wash out at clearance.
2. **Lab-internal AI red-team / model-eval** (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, xAI all hire here). Doesn't require clearance, requires *the skill set that produces public capability writeups*. Build one as a portfolio piece.
3. **Agentic-SOC engineers** ([Exaforce from 2026-05-22/02 §2](../2026-05-22/02-new-emerging.md#2-exaforce), Judgment Labs from [2026-05-13/02](../2026-05-13/02-new-emerging.md), and a wave of next-rounds coming). Pairs **security-graph data engineering + agent eval + cost-aware inference** — the lane where this archive's [`ME.md`](../ME.md) skill investments (agents + MCP + eval + cost) generalize most cleanly into security.

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** **Add "AI red-team / model-eval / agentic-SOC"** as an *adjacent track* to [`ME.md`'s job-search-targeting block](../ME.md#job-search-targeting-as-of-latest-edition) without losing focus on the primary FDE/Solutions lane. The lanes are *complementary*, not competing — they all want the same eval/verification/cost-control skill primitives ([`§3`](#3-eval-as-discipline)).
- **Startup lens:** The **wedge that exploits this**: **commercial tooling that mirrors NSA-style classified eval workflows for enterprise customers** (banks, defense contractors, healthcare). The first mover on a productized version owns the category for 12+ months because the **procurement template is being written right now by federal agencies**, and the company that ships against that template first will be the *de facto* enterprise standard.
- **Insight:** The **rate of lane-creation in 2026 is faster than the rate at which most CS grad-school placement offices can update their job lists.** The asymmetric move is to **track the lane-creation directly** (this archive is exactly that), apply *while the lane is thin*, and avoid waiting for the lane to show up on official career-services rosters. Mythos-adjacent lanes will be on those rosters in Q4 2026; you can apply in June.
