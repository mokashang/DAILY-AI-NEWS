## 1. AI Engineer remains #1 fastest-growing US job title — but it's specialized now {#1-ai-engineer-market}

**The current state of the market (June 2026 data):**

- **AI job postings up 163% YoY** entering 2026; AI Engineer remains the single fastest-growing US job title (since [2026-05-15](../2026-05-15/05-career-and-startup.md), 7 weeks of consistent leadership).
- **~8,931 open AI-engineering positions across 532 companies**; **average ~$228K**, senior IC band **$200K–$312K**.
- **Apple, Google, and TikTok** have the most openings (volume); **Anthropic, OpenAI, Sierra, Decagon** still set the comp ceiling.
- The **AI-skill wage premium** is now **+56% over peers in comparable roles without AI skills** (was +25% one year ago).
- **75%+ of postings require domain depth, not generalist breadth.**
- Python dominates **71% of postings**; **AWS 32.9%, Azure 26%** lead the cloud-AI market.
- Top in-demand skills: **deep learning, LLM fine-tuning, MLOps** — with **LLM fine-tuning** the single most-cited specialized skill in enterprise AI.

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** The 75%-depth number is the directly actionable one. **You cannot apply to AI Engineer roles as a "generalist with ML coursework"** in June 2026 and expect callbacks at the rate you'd have gotten in 2025. The fix is concrete: **pick one sub-specialty and project-evidence it before October.** Your ME.md already names the right one (**AI Integration Engineer / MCP / cost-aware agent design**) — the work this week is to add **one shipped artifact** under that banner (the dual-model sanitiser + the cost log from [`03` §1](./03-practical-skills-and-tools.md#1-reroute) is the natural target). One artifact + one cyber-lane application this week is enough to keep pace.
- **Startup lens:** **The 75%-depth shift cuts both ways for founders.** It means your *engineers* are harder to hire (the bar is higher, the comp is higher), but it also means **a thin, depth-specialized engineer is your best individual capital allocation** — one strong Integration Engineer can do the work of three generalists at this stage. If you're going to hire, hire deep; if you're going to *be* the engineer, *be* deep.
- **Insight:** The market is in the **specialization phase** of every technology cycle. Same shape as web devs 2010–2014 (frontend vs backend vs full-stack collapsed into roles like "React engineer" and "Rails engineer"); same shape as cloud 2014–2018 (sysadmin collapsed into "AWS infrastructure engineer," "Kubernetes platform engineer"). The 2026–2028 collapse of "AI Engineer" into 5+ defined sub-roles is in progress; the sub-roles are roughly: **Inference / MLOps Engineer · AI Integration / FDE · Eval Engineer / AI Safety Eng · AI-Security Engineer · AI Product Engineer**. Pick the one that maps to your interest and your ME.md focusing decision. Re-evaluate at the end of Q3.

2. **The cyber-clearinghouse half of the postponed Trump EO** ([2026-05-22/01 §1](../2026-05-22/01-big-lab-moves.md#1-eo-postponed)) — the part most likely to survive any redraft because it's voluntary, bipartisan-safe, and industry-friendly. ## 3. The midterms-AI political-spending angle (low-priority but worth knowing) {#3-midterms}

**What happened:** Per NPR (June 22, 2026), **groups linked to OpenAI and Anthropic have collectively spent over $15M** on pro- and anti-candidate ads ahead of **today's June 23 primary** — the first cycle in which AI labs (via super PACs) are a *visible* category of political spending. Anthropic-aligned PACs are countering OpenAI-aligned ones, and per the Dario Amodei essay ([`04` §3](./04-research-progress.md#3-amodei-essay)), Anthropic intends to **financially back** policy work substantially.

**Sources:**
- [NPR — Groups tied to OpenAI and Anthropic are spending big on the midterms](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/22/nx-s1-5856359/ai-anthropic-congress-spending-openai-midterms-election) `[secondary]`
- [Dario Amodei — Policy on the AI Exponential](https://darioamodei.com/post/policy-on-the-ai-exponential) `[primary]`

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** **Policy / Government Affairs** at frontier labs is a real role now and the **trust-and-safety / policy track** is, for a CS grad student, a *legitimate non-engineering option* with rising headcount. If you're at all interested in the policy side, today's NPR story is the right moment to send a single LinkedIn cold message to the **Anthropic Policy team** introducing yourself as a technically-grounded grad student interested in pre-deployment-evaluation policy — the lane the postponed EO was about ([2026-05-22/01 §1](../2026-05-22/01-big-lab-moves.md#1-eo-postponed)). Cold-message conversion on policy roles is materially higher than on engineering roles for new grads, because the policy team is smaller and less swamped.
- **Startup lens:** **Don't start an AI-policy startup right now.** The market is rapidly bifurcating into (a) labs running their own policy shops at scale and (b) traditional DC firms picking up AI clients. There's no fundable middle. If you care about policy, do it via a lab or an existing think tank.
- **Insight:** $15M is a small number relative to a midterm cycle, but the **category** is the precedent. When the labs spend $150M next cycle, no one will be surprised, and the AI sector will be a normal political constituency. The pace of normalization here is the second-fastest in tech (only crypto moved faster). Useful as background context for any *"what's the AI landscape going to look like in 2 years"* interview question.
