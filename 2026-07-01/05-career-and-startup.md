## 1. Frontier labs going public — the equity math changes {#1-liquidity}

**What happened (recap of [`01` §3](./01-big-lab-moves.md#3-anthropic-s1)):**

- **Anthropic:** confidential S-1 June 1, October Nasdaq listing target, ~$1T base case, Goldman + JPM + MS.
- **OpenAI:** confidential S-1 filed ~May 22, ~September listing target at $852B–$1T, Goldman + MS.
- **SpaceX (already public):** IPO'd earlier in June, then used its post-IPO stock to acquire Cursor for ~$60B all-stock on June 16.

**What that means for you concretely:**

- **RSUs at a public lab become priceable comp**, not paper. A $400K offer with 50% in equity is now readable as *actually* $400K, minus vesting-and-tax risk (as opposed to the private-company version where you weren't sure what the RSUs would be worth in 4 years).
- **Sarbanes-Oxley discipline** = more predictable L-level ladders, structured hiring windows (quarterly headcount plans), more traceable performance criteria. **Generally good for new-grads and early-career** — the ambiguity that hurts junior hires shrinks.
- **Post-IPO segment disclosure** in the public S-1 (~15 days before roadshow) will tell you which orgs are growing (API vs. Claude.ai vs. Enterprise vs. Solutions/FDE) — that's your **hiring-priority signal by dollar**, not by rumor.
- **Lockup windows create a hiring wave** typically 6-12 months post-IPO — that's Q2-Q4 2027 for OpenAI, Q1-Q3 2028 for Anthropic. If you're not ready to make an offer-decision today, know that the *bigger* hiring wave is coming next year, not this fall.

That changes the *character* of what you're joining — less mission-driven-cult, more quarterly-earnings-discipline. Set your expectations accordingly; that's not a bad thing, but it *is* a different thing.

## 2. The skills reprice — what the FDE job market actually wants right now {#2-skills}

**What the data says (April 2026 snapshot):**

- **FDE / AI-Solutions postings: +729% YoY** — 643 → 5,330 (Indeed). Some trackers cite +1,000% depending on canonicalization.
- **Companies hiring:** Palantir, OpenAI, Anthropic, Scale AI, Cohere, Mistral, Decagon, Sierra, Cresta, Glean, Hebbia, Harvey, Writer + most YC-backed AI-app companies.
- **Comp:** Senior FDE base **$215K–$310K**; frontier-lab total comp regularly **>$500K**; OpenAI FDE mid-level **$160K–$280K base**.
- **AI-Engineer average salary: ~$206K** in the US (+~$50K YoY).
- **LLM specialists: $220K–$280K**; demand +135.8% YoY.
- **Skill mix that shows up in ≥70% of postings:**
  - Core eng (95%+): Python and/or TypeScript, SQL, cloud (AWS/GCP), containers.
  - AI-specific (80%+): LLM app dev, RAG, prompt/eval workflows, agent orchestration.
  - Customer-facing (rising fast, 70%+): "customer empathy," "requirements discovery," "stakeholder management," "translating ambiguous business problems."

**Where this maps to you (adjust the [ME.md](../ME.md) portfolio):**

- **Cost-aware routing** as an explicit skill — write it into your resume that way, not as "used Claude." The Sonnet 5 / GPT-5.6 tiered launches make this **the most re-priced skill of Q3 2026**.
- **Eval-under-drift** — write a project that shows you *catch* provider-side model changes. See [`03` §2](./03-practical-skills-and-tools.md#2-eval-drift). This is the skill hiring managers wish candidates had but almost none do.
- **MCP + hook + skill discipline** — the "narrowest-credential" and "safety-hook" mental models from [`03` §1](./03-practical-skills-and-tools.md#1-sonnet-5-orchestration) are legibly *production-mature*.
- **Domain fluency in one vertical** (from [`02` §2](./02-new-emerging.md#2-legora)) — pick legal, medical coding, insurance, real estate, or freight/logistics. Domain-embedded FDE roles now pay comparably to frontier-lab roles with a much more tractable interview loop.

## 3. The vertical-agent lane {#3-vertical-lane}

**Why it's the calmer path:** Frontier-lab FDE interview loops are 4-7 rounds and heavily selective; vertical-agent-company FDE loops are 2-3 rounds and reward *domain fluency* over research-adjacent depth. Comp is $20K-$60K lower base, but the *ratio of interviews-to-offers* is much better if you can bring one domain.

### Job/Startup/Insight

- **Job lens:** If your CS-grad background has **any** touchpoint to one of the four "under-staked" verticals — even an internship, a family connection, or a coursework project — that's your **highest-leverage application lane this month**. The bar-to-entry for "the AI person who also gets [X]" is much lower than "the AI person, generic."
- **Startup lens:** Pick one of the four under-staked verticals, spend 3 weekends on customer discovery (aim for 10 real conversations with practitioners), and either (a) build a wedge product or (b) walk into the vertical-anchor company's FDE loop with a *disproportionately domain-fluent* application. Both are valid outcomes of the same effort.
- **Insight:** *"Vertical-agent-native at ≥$1B per vertical"* is now a **stable market structure**, not a passing trend. That means the *rate* of new billion-scale vertical AI companies is set by how many verticals still exist that aren't taken — probably 8-12 more in North America. Get on that map early.
