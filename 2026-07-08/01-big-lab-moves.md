# Big Lab Moves — 2026-07-08

Three power moves inside 72 hours, and each shows a different lab reaching for a different lever. **Anthropic reached for the market** — passed OpenAI on revenue and secondary-market valuation, and locked down another 401 MW of US compute with TeraWulf. **OpenAI reached for the state** — proposed a 5% equity donation to a US sovereign wealth fund, a Alaska-Permanent-Fund-style vehicle that would give Washington a permanent stake in the frontier. The frame this week: **the private-market race is being won by the lab you're already investing in on ME.md, while the incumbent's countermove is political rather than technical.**

## 1. Anthropic overtakes OpenAI — on revenue AND secondary valuation {#1-anthropic-crossover}

**What happened:** Two separate firsts confirmed this week:

- **Revenue crossover.** Fortune (Jul 7) reported that **Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI in self-reported revenue**, and is "on course to hit $47B" run-rate with **profitability expected in 2029 — a year ahead of OpenAI's own path.**
- **Secondary-market valuation crossover.** In the same window, **Anthropic surpassed OpenAI in secondary-market valuation for the first time** (private-share trades in the resale market, i.e., what late-stage investors are actually paying).
- **Anthropic's own numbers.** Anthropic's Jul 6 partnership post pegs **run-rate revenue >$30B** — up from **~$9B at end-of-2025**, i.e., roughly **>3× in ~6 months.** Business-customer count spending **>$1M annualized** has passed **1,000** (up from 500 at the February Series G — doubled in <2 months).

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** Your ME.md focusing decision ("committed to the Anthropic agentic stack") is now vindicated by both the revenue and the private markets — **Anthropic is the reference lab of Q3 2026.** Concretely, when you write cover letters or portfolio READMEs, cite the number: "*Anthropic run-rate revenue crossed $30B in Q2 2026 (>3× since December), and >1,000 customers spend >$1M/yr* — I've focused my agentic stack investment on Claude because that's where enterprise standardization is happening." That's a much sharper opener than "I like Claude." Also: this compresses Anthropic's IPO timeline — expect a hiring-freeze-then-pop pattern common to pre-IPO firms; if you're targeting Anthropic Solutions/FDE/Integration ([ME.md](../ME.md#job-search-targeting-as-of-latest-edition)), **apply now** rather than waiting for post-IPO clarity.
- **Startup lens:** A repriced Anthropic changes founder math in three concrete ways: (1) **Claude-as-platform** just became the safer bet vs. OpenAI-as-platform for enterprise-facing products (fewer policy shocks, more enterprise-grade contracting); (2) **secondary tender markets** on Anthropic will open up more employee equity for angel-check recycling, feeding the founder pool; (3) as OpenAI's growth pressure spikes, expect **more OpenAI ads/monetization surface changes** ([2026-05-21/02 §1](../2026-05-21/02-new-emerging.md#1-ads-surface)) — plan for platform-instability risk on that side.
- **Insight:** Read the *cause* of the crossover, not just the fact. It isn't that Anthropic invented anything wildly new — it's that **the enterprise buyer's decision criteria changed to favor Anthropic's shape of product**: predictable pricing, ad-free posture ([2026-05-19/01 ad-free pledge](../2026-05-19/01-big-lab-moves.md)), and Claude Code adoption inside dev orgs. The general lesson: **B2B AI is being decided by procurement rituals, not by benchmark deltas.** When you build or evaluate an AI product, ask "will this survive a security-review + procurement cycle?" before "does this beat X on benchmark Y?"

## 2. Anthropic × TeraWulf — $19B / 20 years / 401 MW in Kentucky {#2-terawulf}

**What happened:** Announced Monday **Jul 6**: Anthropic signed a **20-year lease** on **~401 MW** at TeraWulf's **Justified Data Campus in Hawesville, Kentucky**, expected to generate **~$19B in contracted revenue for TeraWulf** over the initial term.

- **First power: late 2027.** The site is a former aluminum smelting facility on a **790-acre campus** with existing transmission and fiber, which materially speeds construction vs. greenfield.
- **Fluidstack co-transaction.** TeraWulf simultaneously sold a majority interest in its Abernathy JV to Fluidstack — reads as a portfolio cleanup around the Anthropic anchor.
- **Market reaction.** TeraWulf (WULF) opened **+17% premarket** on the news; SEC 8-K filed same-day.
- **Frame within Anthropic's stack.** This slots between the **Google + Broadcom 3.5 GW TPU deal announced Apr 6** (comes online starting 2027) and the **SpaceX/Colossus 1 tenancy** contractually documented at **$1.25B/month through 2029** ([2026-05-21/01 §2](../2026-05-21/01-big-lab-moves.md#2-anthropic-colossus)). Anthropic is deliberately diversifying **across three hardware stacks** (AWS Trainium, Google TPU, NVIDIA GPU) *and* three geographies.

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** Read the geography. **Kentucky, not Texas or Virginia.** Anthropic (and every peer) is pushing compute into **secondary energy markets** where power is cheap, permits move faster, and the political optics of "we're bringing jobs to *your* district" are strongest. The hiring implication: **infra-adjacent roles (site reliability, capacity planning, "AI infra product management")** are increasingly located in cities that don't show up in the standard SF/Seattle/NYC filter. Widen your search radius; a role in Louisville or Cincinnati that pays SF comp is a real 2026 pattern.
- **Startup lens:** 401 MW online in late 2027 = **more inference supply than the market currently has demand for** the moment it lights up. That's a **downward-cost catalyst** for anyone shipping Claude-based products in 2028. Plan margin models with a **~20–30% inference cost tailwind** by then. Also: the *TeraWulf pattern* (a former crypto miner pivots into AI hosting) is repeatable — the next 4–6 crypto-miner-to-AI-DC deals are the leading indicator for regional inference supply.
- **Insight:** The $19B is the *floor*, not the *cost*. Contracted revenue for the operator ≠ Anthropic's total spend (which includes chips, staffing, power). But it's a legible number that **anchors Anthropic's compute obligations to a 20-year horizon**, which changes IPO/S-1 narrative math. The pattern to watch across all frontier labs in 2026 is **very-long-duration compute commitments funded by very-short-duration revenue** — a fragility profile similar to early telecom capex. If revenue growth ever slows, these leases become the flashpoint. Note it for your macro model.

## 3. OpenAI's countermove: a 5% equity donation to a US sovereign wealth fund {#3-openai-swf}

**What happened:** Per an FT scoop (Jul 2), reported out by CNBC/TechCrunch/Time/Forbes/CNN:

- **OpenAI has floated giving ~5% of its equity to a proposed US sovereign wealth fund** modeled on **Alaska's Permanent Fund** (which pays annual dividends from oil revenue to Alaskans).
- **Value at the March round:** ~**$42.6B** on OpenAI's **$852B** post-money.
- **Extended as a category proposal:** Altman is pitching that **Anthropic, Google, and Meta** cede similar stakes, so the fund becomes a public-benefit vehicle spanning the frontier — not a bilateral OpenAI–government deal.
- **Interlocutors:** Altman has raised the concept directly with **President Trump, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.**
- **Precedent this year:** the administration converted federal chip subsidies into a **~10% Intel stake (Aug 2025)** and negotiated **revenue-share deals with NVIDIA and AMD on China-chip sales**. The SWF pitch is the same playbook, applied one layer up.
- **Status:** preliminary; **formal action likely requires congressional approval** — so this is a political signal, not a done deal.

**Sources:**
- [CNBC — OpenAI proposes 5% stake to Trump administration](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/02/openai-proposes-us-government-own-5percent-stake-to-address-political-blowback.html) `[secondary]`
- [TechCrunch — OpenAI proposed donating 5% of its equity to a US sovereign wealth fund](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/02/openai-proposed-donating-5-of-its-equity-to-a-us-sovereign-wealth-fund/) `[secondary]`
- [Time — OpenAI woos Trump administration as investor](https://time.com/article/2026/07/03/openai-invest-ai-trump-administration-sam-altman/) `[secondary]`

- [CNN Business — OpenAI in talks to give Trump administration a 5% stake](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/02/business/openai-trump-stake-intl) `[secondary]`

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** If any version of this closes, **frontier-lab compensation gets a permanent political dimension.** Equity grants at OpenAI (and probably Anthropic/Google/Meta) would be issued against a cap table with a **US-government-controlled block**, which changes the shape of secondary-tender events, employee liquidity, and the tenor of internal debate about policy work. Concrete action: when you evaluate offers this fall, **read the equity-vesting docs for "government stake" language** the same way you'd read for cliff length. Not just curiosity — a legal question.
- **Startup lens:** A precedent where the state takes a permanent equity slice in "critical" AI companies is **a headwind for late-stage exits** — public-market IPOs get harder to price when a chunk of the cap table is a wealth fund. Downstream: **more strategic M&A, fewer IPOs**, and — importantly for founders — **more Big-4-lab acqui-hires** as an exit path. Align your wedge, when possible, to be *acquirable* by a frontier lab (integration surface, data pipeline, or benchmark lift they need internally), not just IPO-able.
- **Insight:** This is the shape of the **Trump-postponed-the-EO** story ([2026-05-22/01 §1](../2026-05-22/01-big-lab-moves.md#1-eo-postponed)) landing on the other side. Pre-release review died as a regulatory concept; the SWF is what replaces it — **not oversight in the safety sense, but ownership in the equity sense.** In 2026, US AI policy is being written in the language of cap tables, not safety cases. Every "safety" pitch you make in interviews or job apps should be paired with a story about **who gets paid**, because that's the frame the current administration understands.
