## 2. Model fatigue reshapes the resume-decay curve — bet on capabilities, not SKUs {#2-model-fatigue-lens}

**What happened:** CNBC's 2026-09-06 "model fatigue" framing — four labs, four flagship model events, one week — has a direct career implication. The half-life of a **specific model SKU on your resume** has dropped from ~9 months (2024) to **~6 weeks (2026)**. Anyone who claimed "Fable 5 expertise" in July 2026 has a **Fable 5.1** update to metabolize by September, and probably a **Fable 5.2** by Christmas.

**Sources:**
- [CNBC — 'Model fatigue' sets in as AI labs race to roll out new versions](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/06/meta-google-openai-anthropic-ai-model-fatigue.html) `[secondary]`
- [Pragmatic Engineer — State of the software engineering job market in 2026](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/state-of-the-job-market-2026) `[analysis]`

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** **Move your resume from SKU-specific to capability-specific.** *"Claude Fable 5.1 expert"* is not a great line in December when Fable 5.2 has shipped. **"Effort-routing + cache-first agent design + verifier composition"** is a great line **for the next 24 months**. Rewrite your top 5 bullets tonight.
- **Startup lens:** Same principle for your **pitch deck** — do not name specific SKUs in your architecture slide (they decay); name **capabilities and interfaces** (they persist). Bake **model routing** into your product architecture so you can swap SKUs weekly without customer breakage.
- **Insight:** The **strategic value of "capabilities-first" thinking** compounds. Every time a new model ships, someone who wrote their resume against a specific SKU has to update, while someone who wrote it against a capability has to update the *supporting numbers* but not the frame. The frame stays fresh. That's a small but real edge over 3–5 years.

## 4. Anthropic + OpenAI IPO windows — run your RSU-vs-options math this weekend {#4-ipo-equity-math}

**What happened:** Both frontier labs targeting a Sept–Oct 2026 listing window. Anthropic reportedly $2T valuation ceiling per NYT; OpenAI targeting ~$1T+. **Quiet periods** started or starting soon at both.

**Sources:**
- [Yahoo Finance — Anthropic Is Reportedly Planning to Unveil IPO Prospectus After Labor Day](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/anthropic-reportedly-planning-unveil-ipo-151235633.html) `[secondary]`
- [Financial Samurai — IPO Quiet Period Explained: What Anthropic Can And Cannot Say](https://www.financialsamurai.com/ipo-quiet-period/) `[analysis]`
- [BitMEX — Anthropic IPO Guide: Price, Date, and Valuation](https://www.bitmex.com/blog/anthropic-ipo-guide) `[analysis]`

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** If you have a **live interview loop** at Anthropic or OpenAI: push to close **before public S-1 makes the option strike public**. Post-IPO, offer letters shift from options to RSUs; RSUs are cleaner but have a real vesting cliff and often lower upside than pre-IPO options. Ask explicitly on your final call: *"Is my offer being structured against pre-IPO options or post-IPO RSUs?"*
- **Startup lens:** Once public, both companies will file quarterly 10-Qs — **revenue by segment, gross margin, customer concentration**. That's *free* competitive intelligence you can act on. Read every 10-Q the day it drops; adapt your positioning.
- **Insight:** The **RSU-vs-options math** for a lab pre-IPO is a real spreadsheet, not vibes:
  - **Pre-IPO options:** strike price ≈ last 409A (typically well below IPO price), but you pay AMT on exercise and taxes on gain at IPO — the *pre-tax gross* looks bigger but the *after-tax net* often isn't.
  - **Post-IPO RSUs:** grant value = share count × current stock price; taxes withheld at vest at ordinary income; simple but capped by the current market.
  - **A conservative pre-IPO offer at 1.5× the RSU equivalent is roughly break-even** after AMT, tax friction, and lockup risk. Below 1.5× → take the RSU. Above 2× → the options are meaningfully better.

  Run this math yourself before signing anything. There are free calculators (Secfi, Compound); use one.

**Sources:**

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** If you have any **DoD adjacency** (family clearance, ROTC, defense internship, physics/nuclear coursework, cleared previous work), lean into it. The **clearance-eligible + coding-strong** profile is very rare and pays a substantial premium — often $50–100K on top of the equivalent uncleared FDE band. Even if you're not cleared, US citizenship + willingness to pursue clearance is a real filter that shrinks the applicant pool.
- **Startup lens:** The DoD/frontier-lab contracting pattern is a **template that will replicate** across allied governments (UK, Australia, Japan, Israel, EU member states). If you build a red-team-for-agents product, structuring around the DoD contract line (pre-deployment eval + adversarial-prompt testing + audit-log verification) makes you *export-compatible* — same product sells to five governments with modest localization.
- **Insight:** The **safety / eval / assurance job category** is now a durable career lane, not a transient concern. The FOIA docs show that government procurement will require **structured pre-deployment eval** as a line item for the foreseeable future. If you have alignment/eval interest but also want a paid job, the private-sector *eval-for-federal-contracts* seat is the answer.
