## 3. The under-priced risk: 24% customer concentration + Pentagon break {#3-concentration-risk}

**What's new today:** Yesterday's edition framed the Pentagon break (Oct 5) as a clean career signal. What the S-1 leak adds — and the Oct 14 investor day will have surfaced — is that **nearly 24% of Anthropic's 2025 revenue came from just TWO customers.**

Business Times (per the leaked filing) reported the concentration figure; neither customer is named publicly. The reasonable candidate set — given disclosed partnerships — is some combination of:

- **AWS** (hosts Anthropic's models + resells; marketplace revenue flows back)
- **Google** (minority investor + major reseller via Vertex)
- **A top-4 consulting partner** (McKinsey / Deloitte / Accenture / Bain — now also Frontier Academy partners)
- **A federal-adjacent reseller** (Palantir, Carahsoft, or similar — the one that would have carried DoD revenue before the Oct 5 break)

### The under-priced reading

If one of those two customers has material DoD exposure — a federal-reseller profile, say — **the Pentagon break's revenue impact is 2–3× what the clean "DoD ceased product use" narrative implies**, because the pass-through contract through a reseller may also unwind. That's not a public claim; it's a plausible lower bound once concentration data is overlaid on the Pentagon news.

### The counter-reading

- Q2 2026 ARR crosses $30B (per April reporting). Even if concentration was 24% in CY2025, by the IPO filing date it is likely <15% simply because the numerator is growing faster than any single customer can grow with it.
- The "two customers" could be AWS + Google — both pure platform resellers, with no DoD-specific exposure, in which case the Pentagon break is immaterial to concentration risk.
- Anthropic has already said it would challenge any supply-chain-risk designation in court, which means the DoD question has a legal exit lane.

## 4. Claude Code v2.1.294 (Oct 8) — hook-interpretation fix {#4-cc-2-1-294}

**What happened (2026-10-08):** Per havoptic.com's third-party release aggregator (so take as `[aggregator]` not `[primary]`), Claude Code v2.1.294 fixed how instruction-based hooks are interpreted so blocking rules work as intended. This follows **v2.1.293 (Oct 7)** making Haiku 5.5 (1M context) the default model — covered in yesterday's edition.

**Why it matters to you:**
- If your `.claude/hooks` configuration has blocking rules (e.g., "block any write to `secrets/*`"), re-test them against v2.1.294. Before this fix, a subset of instruction-string-matched blocking rules were silently permissive — meaning your "safety hook" may not have been blocking what you thought it was.
- Together with Haiku-5.5-as-default, the Claude Code stack is now self-consistent for the first time since the late-September cadence. Weekend plan: **router artifact rebuild** targeting Haiku-5.5-as-worker (per yesterday's edition), then **MCP stateless migration** (next section).
