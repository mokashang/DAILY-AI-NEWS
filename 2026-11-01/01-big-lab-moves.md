## 2. Google ships Gemini 4 Carbon — retakes the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index #1 at 59, ~half the price of Opus 5.5, cyber-defender-staged launch {#2-gemini-4-carbon}

**What happened (2026-10-28 / 29):** Google unveiled **Gemini 4 Carbon**, scoring **59 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0** — the top score, ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 (58), GPT-6 Astra (57), and Gemini 4 Argon (50). The launch is a **staged rollout, "limited at launch to a subset of cyber defenders"** — Google is rationing frontier capability by vertical, not just price.

**Numbers:**
- **AA Intelligence Index v5.0:** Gemini 4 Carbon **59** · Claude Opus 5.5 **58** · GPT-6 Astra **57** · Gemini 4 Argon **50**
- **Pricing:** **$4 input / $20 output per 1M tokens standard**; **$2 / $10 intro window**
- **AA blended cost per 1M tokens:** $2.94
- **Cybersecurity benchmarks:** SEC-bench Pro **88.2%** · ExploitGym **54.8%** · CWE-bench **71.0%**
- **Context (previous Gemini 4 Argon release, late Sept 2026):** AutomationBench 51.3%, LVBench 91.7%, DeepSWE v1.1 77.9%

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens — rebuild your routing policy with Carbon as a tier this weekend.** The Opus 5.5 "most-capable" default now has a 23% cheaper challenger that scores one Intelligence Index point higher. Opus's routing threshold shifts *down*: tasks that previously routed to Opus can route to Carbon if (a) the use case tolerates Google's data posture, (b) the latency profile matches, (c) your tenant has access (today, only cyber-defender tenants do — this will expand). **Resume language upgrade:** "I maintain a provider-neutral router over Haiku 5.5 / Sonnet / Opus / Gemini 4 Carbon / GPT-6 Astra, with per-task-class routing and a continuous-cost dashboard" is now the baseline FDE-interview answer. Four months ago it was differentiating; today it's expected.
- **Startup lens — the "cyber defender first" vertical-rationing is the new launch pattern.** Google rationed access to Carbon by *defensive security role*, not by price or geography — which gives every security-adjacent startup a 60-day arbitrage window. Three wedges:
  - **(a) "Carbon-for-non-cyber bridging"** — enterprises that *are* in the staged list sell Carbon-mediated services to peers that aren't. Niche but immediate; 6-month half-life.
  - **(b) Benchmark-slice specialists** — Carbon's **54.8% ExploitGym** is a stated weakness relative to its own SEC-bench Pro 88.2%. A startup that specializes in agent-driven exploit discovery (CTF-style, red-team-adjacent) can out-perform in the specific slice.
  - **(c) Cross-provider cyber-eval harnesses** — the Carbon vs. Opus 5.5 cyber comparison needs third-party verification; shipping an open-source eval suite that scores *all three* on identical workflows is the kind of neutral utility a frontier lab will try to acquire.
- **Insight — the frontier is now priced like a commodity with a reputation premium.** Carbon at $4/$20 and Opus 5.5 at $?/? (undisclosed in the window; implied higher) with one Intelligence Index point between them means **the model-choice question is now a provider-trust-and-posture question, not a capability one.** This structurally reprices "which Claude" down (you already knew) and also "which lab" (you didn't). The *second-order* effect lands at Anthropic's roadshow door: an underwriter running sensitivity on "Anthropic revenue if 20% of frontier workloads route to Carbon within 6 months" is now a believable scenario in the S-1 memo. **Watch for an Anthropic model release inside the roadshow window** — Claude Opus 5.5+ or Sonnet 5.5+ — explicitly to re-top the index and reset the narrative before pricing. ### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens — a published Agent Skill is now a Linux-Foundation-badge OSS contribution on your résumé.** That is a materially different line than "I wrote a skill for Claude Code" — it moves from "I used a product" to "I contributed to a Linux Foundation project." The specific move: **publish one Agent Skill this month that covers a workflow you actually do** (e.g., `arxiv-digest`, `pr-triage`, `mcp-registry-submit`, `s1-reader`). Keep it small (3 instructions, 2 scripts, 1 reference doc) and name it well. Then list it on your résumé as `Agent Skill contributor, Agentic AI Foundation (hosts MCP + 10 other projects)`. For an Anthropic Applied AI Engineer loop, this is a 5-minute talking point that lands.

The *reason* this works commercially is that it hard-codes Anthropic's *authorship* reputation while removing lock-in objections for enterprise buyers. **If you're pitching an agent-ecosystem startup**, your deck should have a slide answering "what's your authorship-and-donation path?" because the sophisticated LPs on your cap table are now reading this as a required strategy loop, not an idealistic nice-to-have. - **Insight — the Agent Skills + MCP + ACP triad is now a layered standards stack with the Linux Foundation as neutral ground.** MCP is the tool-call protocol, ACP (Agent Client Protocol, Zed-led) is the editor-agent protocol, and Agent Skills is the capability-packaging protocol. The three overlap but don't collide. **For portfolio depth, pick one layer and go deep rather than skimming all three.** Your committed path (Anthropic + MCP) now has an upstream (Agent Skills, authorship-tier contribution) and a downstream (ACP, consumption-tier integration). **Build a workflow that uses MCP ↔ Agent Skill ↔ ACP end-to-end** — it's a 1-weekend demo that reads as "I understand the whole stack."
