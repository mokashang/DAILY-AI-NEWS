## 4. "ANT day-one trading thesis" — the 10-minute drafting prompt for Monday interviews {#4-ant-interview-thesis}

**The scenario:** Monday Nov 9 is roadshow kickoff. Every FDE / Solutions / Policy recruiter at Anthropic is refreshing EDGAR every 2 hours. Every lab-adjacent interviewer this week will want to know **what you think of the deal.** This is the single most-reliable conversation starter for the entire week; having a 2-paragraph answer in your pocket is the difference between "interesting candidate" and "we'll move forward."

**The 10-minute drafting prompt (do this tonight, before you ship the artifact):**

```
Write a 2-paragraph "ANT day-one trading thesis" that addresses:

1. What price range I think the amended S-1 should land in. Anchor to:
   - H1 2026 revenue: $16.3B (Oct 26 S-1)
   - H1 2026 net loss: $80.7B (mostly non-cash GPU-contract remeasurement)
   - Approximate run-rate operating burn: [your estimate; cite the reasoning]
   - NRR: ~900% (from the Oct 29 edition's enterprise-accounts detail)
   - Investor-reported walk-down: $1.1T–$1.3T (vs. $2T a month ago)

2. Which of the three Monday events is the biggest risk, and why:
   - Price-range filing (if it lands above $1.3T or below $1.1T, what does that signal?)
   - Antitrust complaint response (if the amended S-1 moves it to "material," the band compresses)
   - METR redaction (if an analyst asks on the call, what's the right lab answer?)

End with: "If I were advising a potential ANT buyer at the roadshow, the
one question I'd tell them to ask is: '[your question].'"
```

**Example "one question":** *"What is Anthropic's run-rate operating burn with the SpaceX / Colossus GPU-contract remeasurement stripped out, in both Q2 and Q3 2026, and how does that compare to projected revenue in the four quarters covered by the roadshow forecasts?"*

**Save it as a note in your interview-prep system.** Use the first line of the thesis as your opening answer whenever an interviewer asks "what do you think of the IPO?" — they'll remember the specific question more than the thesis itself, because specific-and-grounded is the signature of someone who's spent time in the problem.
