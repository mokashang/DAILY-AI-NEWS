**What happened (2026-09 → 2026-11-09):**
- **Financial Times reported Nov 9** that Anthropic agreed in **late September 2026** to assume **OnePWR's prior order from Caterpillar** for **1.7 GW of heavy-duty natural-gas generators**, paying **~$3.4B** (about **$2M per MW** including engineering + installation).
- **OnePWR had ordered the generators ~2 years ago but couldn't secure financing** to close; Anthropic takes over both the equipment order and the deployment.
- **Anthropic's Nov 2 amended S-1/A-1 discloses on-site gas generation at planned data-center sites** — the first time a frontier lab has filed a "we will generate our own power off-grid at gigawatt scale" strategy.
- **Pairs with xAI's Memphis air-quality NAACP lawsuit** — permitting, not procurement, is now the binding constraint on new compute.

## 4. California unfair-competition suit now names Anthropic + Google + Meta + OpenAI + SpaceX AI unit jointly (Nov 12) {#4-ca-ucl-suit}

**What happened (2026-11-12):**
- A California unfair-competition law (UCL, Business & Professions Code § 17200) suit filed **Nov 12, 2026** names as co-defendants **Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, and an AI unit of SpaceX** (presumably xAI / the post-acquisition Cursor stack).
- **Single-claim** complaint — unusual for a multi-defendant tech case; standard scope-expansion tactic in UCL filings.
- **No class action yet**, but UCL plaintiffs can seek restitution + injunctive relief under state statute.

**Sources:**
- [Just Security — AI labs face joint-defendant California UCL suit (summary via Industry.co.id roundup)](https://www.industry.co.id/read/159207/rangkuman-update-ai-global-15-november-2026-lab-ai-digugat) `[aggregator]`

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** **"Litigation-ready engineer"** (deposition-aware documentation, version-controlled eval artifacts, immutable audit logs) becomes a hireable sub-skill at every named lab. This was previously a safety-team concern — it's now **a platform-engineering concern** because the underlying claim is about *how* models are shipped, not their outputs.
- **Startup lens:** **The joint-defendant template is new.** State AGs now have a playbook to sue the whole frontier-lab set together — which means **individual labs lose the ability to settle out quietly**. For a downstream startup, this hardens the case to **log everything through your own eval harness** (so you have your own chain of custody separate from the vendor's).
- **Insight:** **The Nov 12 suit + NY RAISE Act Jan 1 + federal EO on state AI rules** form a three-front compliance picture. Whichever framework prevails, "safety-framework author + incident-response lead + litigation-ready ML eng" is a career lane. **Expect big-law + Big 4 practice groups to hire specifically for this** through Q1 2027.

## 5. New York RAISE Act — compliance clock starts Jan 1, 2027 {#5-ny-raise-act}

**What happened (signed late-2025 / amended March 2026; compliance from 2027-01-01):**
- **Scope:** developers with **>$500M annual revenue** training frontier AI models.
- **72-hour critical-incident reporting** to the NY AG's office (down to **24 hours for imminent risk of death / serious injury**).
- **Published safety frameworks + transparency reports + disclosure filings** required annually from Jan 1, 2027.
- **Penalties:** up to **$1M for first violations, $3M for subsequent**.
- **Reporting speed: faster than California's 15-day SB 1047-successor window.**
- **Federal tension:** a federal executive order limiting state AI rules creates pre-emption uncertainty — status unresolved as of today.

**Sources:**

- [Fisher Phillips — NY Governor signs sweeping AI safety law](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/new-york-governor-signs-sweeping-ai-safety-law) `[analysis]`
- [legal.io — NY RAISE Act signals state-level AI safety standards](https://www.legal.io/blog/5774957/New-York-s-RAISE-Act-Signals-a-Push-Toward-State-Level-AI-Safety-Standards) `[analysis]`
- [eWEEK — NY Governor Seeks AI Safety Via RAISE Act Signing](https://www.eweek.com/news/ny-governor-signs-raise-act/) `[secondary]`

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** A new job family crystallizes: **"safety-framework author / incident-response lead / transparency-report engineer"** at every lab > $500M rev. Expect **5–15 new reqs per frontier lab** (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, xAI, Mistral) before Dec 31. **Résumé signal:** compliance or audit coursework + one pre-deployment-eval open-source artifact (even a small one) + a writeup of how to run a 72-hour-SLA incident triage. This is a **non-ML-research AI-safety job** — doable from a CS grad program.
- **Startup lens:** **Compliance-as-a-service** for mid-size AI vendors ($500M–$5B rev) is the clearest 2027 B2B SaaS wedge. Products: **safety-framework templates, 72-hour incident-reporting portals, automated transparency-report generation, model-card + eval-artifact versioning**. Target customers: the ~30 labs that will cross the $500M threshold in 2027 but don't have Big 4 relationships yet.
- **Insight:** **72 hours is faster than any software security incident response SLA we've seen in US tech regulation.** It forces labs to **pre-build the triage process** (not just the team). The labs that already run red-team + capability-eval playbooks (Anthropic, OpenAI) get a procedural head-start; smaller labs (Mistral, Cohere, open-weight distributors) get squeezed.
