**What happened:**

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** **Your negotiation anchor is $400K for new grads at the top, not the $120K FAANG default from 2023.** Prep the specific companies that have paid at or near that band (ask recruiters directly in screening calls) and lead with the Entrepreneur citation + a specific Levels.fyi profile. **The gap between what you ask for and what you get is usually 15–25% of the gap between your ask and theirs** — so ask at the top of band and negotiate down.
- **Startup lens:** **If you're founding or founding-eng, your comp conversation with early investors needs to account for a new-grad at $250–400K being competitive now.** Raise accordingly; budget ~$5–8M of your seed on talent alone if you plan a 5-person team by month 12.
- **Insight:** **Compensation is the clearest market signal in AI.** When a market is irrationally exuberant, comp bands peak and then compress fast. The current new-grad premium has lasted ~18 months and is still widening at the top — that suggests at least one more year of expansion before the top of band flattens. Lock in your offer now, not in Q3 2027.

## 5. VC thesis note: "agents as employees" + "services, not software" + "avoid connector wrappers" {#5-vc-thesis}

**What happened (no new Nov 14–21 post surfaced; prevailing consensus):**
- **a16z — "Avoiding Death on the Yellow Brick Road"**: against basic-connector wrapper startups. Thesis: if the connector value can be replicated with 50 lines of an agent SDK, there's no moat.
- **Sequoia (Julien Bek) — "Services: The New Software"**: software budgets flatten, labor budgets expand; AI startups should price into the labor line.
- **Portfolio reality check**: a16z/Sequoia/Bessemer/Greylock/Insight are still betting on LangChain, Glean, Sierra, Dust, Decagon, Harvey, Model ML, BrightAI. The theses are consistent.
- **Watch**: **Noeri's "living systems"** framing (see `02` §2) is likely to show up in VC memos through Q1 2027 as the new category framing for agent-ops.

**Sources:**
- [a16z — Avoiding Death on the Yellow Brick Road](https://a16z.com/avoiding-death-on-the-yellow-brick-road/) `[primary]`
- [Linas on Sequoia (Julien Bek) — Services: The New Software](https://linas.substack.com/p/sequoiathesis) `[secondary]`
- [AgentMarketCap — VC AI agent investment thesis](https://agentmarketcap.ai/blog/2026/04/09/vc-ai-agent-investment-thesis-2024-2026-bessemer-a16z-sequoia) `[aggregator]`

### Why it matters to you

- **Job lens:** **Read these three posts before any VC-backed-startup interview this quarter.** The questions "how do you avoid wrapping?" and "what's the labor-budget line item you're displacing?" are now routine from engineering managers (not just investors). Having a specific answer with a specific $-amount is a differentiator.
- **Startup lens:** **If you're pitching this quarter, your deck needs a slide named "where we aren't the yellow brick road."** Name the connector you're not, the moat you are, and the eval you can show a buyer that proves it. VCs will ask explicitly — have the answer pre-written.
- **Insight:** **The "services, not software" framing is probably correct in 2027 and probably wrong in 2029.** Build your startup to collect services revenue first (consulting, custom FDE engagements, revenue-share), then migrate to productized subscription once you have 20+ customers on the same shape of problem. The 2029 return to "software" happens when the product is finally generalizable; getting there via services is the fast path.
